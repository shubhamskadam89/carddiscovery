Card discovery: results, machine load and what improved

All runs used the real Gmail API against a test inbox of 10,000 made-up but realistic emails covering 5 years: 12 cards plus an add-on card, upgrades, replacements, a closure, dormant cards, 2,489 promotions, 668 debit/savings emails and scam traps. Emails were imported into the test inbox, never sent. Server: API, 2 workers and a scheduler on a MacBook Air, Postgres 18.

How it improved
  • Filter noise in the search itself, which costs nothing. Promotions tab, OTP, debit and savings subjects and offers are excluded before any email is read. Of 10,000 emails, 600 were read at first, now about 170.
  • Search known card numbers instead of reading their mail. A search costs 5 units and a read 20. Once a card is known, every later search excludes its number, and its history comes from a few searches by number.
  • Time windows, newest first. Recent windows find the cards in use; older windows only surface cards that went quiet. Each new card is looked up right away and never searched again.
  • Checked on real Gmail: searching "2846" does not match "XX2846". Each number is searched in its masked forms, plus the forms learned from the user's own mail.
  • Add-on cards: each card's newest statement is opened in full.
  • Someone else's card (another name on every email) is hidden.
  • Statement PDFs are indexed per card but not downloaded until PDF analysis is built.

Open issues
  • Fixed Forged bank mail. At 3 fake emails for every real one in the last year, forged "upgrade" and "replacement" mail had moved 5 real cards to history and 267 fake numbers showed up for review. Now only bank mail that Gmail itself verified (DMARC/DKIM for the bank's domain) counts; big status changes need follow-up evidence; one-off cards wait to be confirmed. Result: 9/9 cards, nothing to review. Cost: each fake from a bank domain is read once (20 units) to find out it's fake, so the scan took 28,104 units. In real inboxes most such forgeries never arrive: 11 of 12 bank domains tell Gmail to reject them (DMARC p=reject); Kotak doesn't.
  • Next Cheaper scans under a flood (skip numbers seen only in fake mail), fakes that pass Gmail's check (a bank's own marketing, phishing-style mail from a bank system, unlisted bank domains), and fixing the slow job start.
  • Not proven yet Real bank mail. All of this is on made-up emails we wrote. The next real signal is 20–30 real team inboxes.
  • Not explained The quick job sometimes spends 18–33 s before its 3-second scan, probably Google token refresh or network. Needs profiling.
